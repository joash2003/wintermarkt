## Assessment details and inconsistencies

Kickoff p. 9 allocates 50 game-design, 25 technology, 15 playtesting and 10 creativity points. The detailed p. 10 rubric emphasizes coherent presentation, fluid controls, balanced difficulty, player feedback and theory-grounded decisions. Its strongest playtesting category expects documentation of both class playtesting sessions and evidence that results affected the final game.

There are internal discrepancies: p. 9 shows zero points for insufficient game design while p. 10 shows 15; average creativity is 7 on p. 9 but 3 on p. 10. The detailed technology rubric says an unplayable game can receive zero for the whole game. Confirm ambiguous scoring with teaching staff; do not promise a grade. The practical priority is a stable playable game and evidence of iteration.

## Applying the design workshop to Linz Wintermarkt

- **MDA (p. 4):** placing/upgrading buildings and queue rules are mechanics; crowds, bottlenecks and investment patterns are dynamics; cozy bustle and satisfaction from improving the market are intended emotional outcomes.
- **Formal elements (pp. 6–7):** one manager/player; maximize unique visitors; place, inspect and upgrade; spend coins and limited space; resolve demand versus capacity; a bounded square and festival duration; measurable visitor score.
- **Dramatic elements (p. 8):** a Christmas-market organizer building a welcoming local celebration. The setting supports the mechanics without requiring a large story.
- **Dynamic elements (p. 9):** attraction raises arrivals, queues affect visitor experience, reputation influences later arrivals, income funds improvements. These relationships must be observed in playtests.
- **Challenge (pp. 13–15):** let players pause and inspect; introduce systems gradually. Test whether crowd pressure is enjoyable rather than frustrating.
- **Meaningful decisions (pp. 16–17):** a ride attracts visitors, while a service upgrade relieves queues. Show costs and effects clearly. Neither option should always dominate.
- **Iteration and scope (pp. 18–22):** validate one complete stall/visitor/upgrade loop, then expand. Change a small number of tuning values per test.
- **Prototype practice (pp. 29–34):** diagram the core loop, use minimal art/sound first, and test controls, mechanics and technical feasibility separately.
